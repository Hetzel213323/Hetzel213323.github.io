Growth Mindset

After automation ends her career, Maya follows an unlikely spark: a stubborn sign of life growing through the ruins of a broken wall, into five years of trial, failure, and reinvention.

A film by Matt Hetzel · Submitted to the Future Vision XPRIZE

Watch the film Read the treatment
The Story

Not a story about technology replacing humans.

Growth Mindset is a story about what happens after the ground shifts, and what we choose to grow in the rubble. The disruption in this film is real. The fear is valid. The technology is neither villain nor savior. It is simply present, as ordinary and disruptive as electricity once was. What is extraordinary is what Maya does with the space it opens up.

I began making this film before Peter Diamandis announced the Future Vision XPRIZE. When I encountered his vision of an abundant future, something clarified. I was already building exactly this, because I believe the stories we tell about the future shape the future we build.

- Matt Hetzel, filmmaker
Act One / The Ground Shifts

She packed her desk carefully.

Maya does not fall apart in public. She loads her plant into a cardboard box with the precision of someone who has always shown up and delivered, and walks the length of an empty corridor for the last time. The months that follow are not a portrait of individual failure. They are a portrait of collective displacement. Conference halls fill with government retraining seminars. Podcast voices bleed from passing cars. The world is simply faster than anyone prepared for.

The grinding pressure fractures her relationship with her first partner, a fundamentally good person caught in the same tide. Their story does not end in betrayal. It ends in attrition. Then, on a construction site, something stops her: a wild tomato plant pushing through a crack in a crumbling wall. Where others see a weed, Maya sees a system. The building crumbled. The plant didn't ask permission to keep growing.

Act Two / The Synthesis

She didn't have an epiphany. She had a pause.

That pause is the whole film. Using AI as a collaborative learning partner (not a magic answer), Maya teaches herself the science of vertical farming and living architecture. She wants to bring organic life into the concrete infrastructure of cities. The work is iterative, messy, and joyful in a way her previous roles never were. She fails constantly. She keeps going.

During this phase she meets her new partner, a man with beautifully integrated cybernetic enhancements that he initially tries to conceal. They find each other in a shared worldview: he is augmenting his body to adapt to the future, while she is augmenting the physical world to sustain it. Biology and technology integrated, producing something better than either alone.

Act Three / What We Grow

Five years later, the city has changed.

Maya has not won an award or given a speech. She has built something real alongside other people who were also freed by disruption and necessity to finally do what they were meant for. The vertical farm greenhouse she helped design is now a place visitors walk through. Children learn in a living outdoor classroom. On a rooftop, people eat food that was grown two floors below them, and nobody is in a hurry.

On a recent Tuesday, Maya slept the entire afternoon and called it a victory. In the world of Act One, that would have been a confession of failure. Here, it is something close to a brag. The disruption is real. The fear was valid. This film refuses to pretend otherwise. It simply insists that what waits on the other side was worth the crossing.

Production

This film is a demonstration of its own thesis.

Every tool named below was used exactly as Maya uses AI in the story: as a collaborative partner, not a replacement.

Google Veo via Google Flow

Primary video generation across Maya's full five-year arc: vertical farms, holographic design prototypes, rooftop dinners, and the film's closing sequences. Veo 3.1 via Google Flow with Gemini Omni Flash for character consistency. The Veo watermark is deliberately retained throughout the film as a transparent indicator of tool provenance. The technology made this possible. Naming it is part of the film's argument.

Adobe Firefly

Visual concept development and select imagery for Act Three's warmer register: the golden-hour garden sequences and the biophilic city aesthetic that defines the film's closing vision.

ElevenLabs

Maya's voice, cloned and developed inside ElevenLabs using a source voice that matched her character profile. The voice heard on this page and in the film was generated using Eleven v3. Post-production used Adobe Premiere Pro for initial assembly and DaVinci Resolve for color grading and final output.

AI Collaboration

Two AI systems helped write the story.

Neither Claude nor Gemini generated this film's core ideas. Both helped sharpen and challenge them. Claude served as story consultant and critical reader across multiple drafts of the treatment, pushing on narrative coherence, character consistency, and thematic clarity. Gemini served as creative collaborator for image generation, voice development, and visual production decisions.

The distinction matters: AI as accelerant, not author. This film argues that the most beneficial human-AI collaboration is one where the machine makes room for us to think better, not one where it thinks for us. The making of this film was an attempt to live that argument out loud.

Logline

After automation ends her corporate career, Maya follows a wild plant growing through broken concrete into a five-year journey of retraining, living architecture, and human reinvention.

Synopsis

Growth Mindset follows Maya over a transformative five-year period beginning in the immediate aftermath of sudden career disruption caused by automation. Rather than framing technology as an existential adversary, the film explores the disorientation of displacement alongside the possibility of human renewal.

In Act One, Maya navigates the emotional and practical toll of career loss. Retraining seminars and public exhaustion reflect a society in transition. A chance encounter with a wild plant thriving inside a demolished wall reorients her perspective on resilience.

In Act Two, Maya uses generative and predictive AI tools as accessible learning collaborators to teach herself vertical farming and living architecture. She encounters her partner, who models human cybernetic augmentation. Together, they synthesize biology and technology into restorative urban design.

Act Three reveals a mature, flourishing community vertical greenhouse. Maya and her family live in an abundant, biophilic city where human time is reclaimed for rest, shared meals, and connection.

Personal Statement

As a UX designer and accessibility specialist with a background in counseling psychology, I have spent fourteen years helping people navigate technology transitions. Too often, sci-fi defaults to dystopian fear or frictionless fantasy. Growth Mindset was created to honor the authentic friction of change while showing that human judgment, empathy, and nature-integrated technology can produce a deeply desirable future worth building.
